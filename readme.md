Abstract: Addressing the issue of UAV formation control under GPS-restricted environments, this paper proposes a collaborative relative positioning (RL) method that is independent of infrastructure. This method abandons the visual-based relative coordinate estimation approach and employs an onboard ultra-wideband (UWB) ranging and communication (RCM) network to simultaneously achieve distance perception and position information interaction between UAVs in two-dimensional space. Introduction: In recent years, multi-UAV system formation control has become a popular research direction due to its wide applications in surveillance, forest monitoring, disaster relief, and other fields. However, most formation control methods require the acquisition of relative positions between UAVs as a prerequisite. Additionally, UAVs in formation applications must be efficient, with their payload, perception capabilities, and computational resources all being limited. Therefore, how to estimate relative positions with fewer resources and complete flight formation remains a valuable research topic.
The traditional methods for obtaining relative positioning typically rely on communication networks, where drones need to know their global position in a shared coordinate system and communicate with other drones to infer their relative positions based on shared data. These methods have achieved certain results in simulations and actual flights (especially quadrotor drones), but they all depend on external positioning infrastructure such as Global Positioning System (GPS), motion tracking systems, or radio-based positioning networks anchored by beacons (e.g., Ultra Wideband). However, the reliance on these infrastructures has significant drawbacks: GPS's reliability is insufficient in complex urban environments, alternative solutions require careful planning of operation areas and limited coverage, and the precision of infrastructure (such as beacon positions, camera calibration) can significantly affect positioning accuracy. In unfamiliar environments like forests, the difficulty of infrastructure deployment is enormous, making such positioning systems unsuitable for exploration tasks in unknown environments.
Moreover, existing non-infrastructure-dependent positioning methods also have numerous shortcomings: Visual-based methods are limited by perspective, occlusion, and lighting conditions, and they involve massive computation; Synchronous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) methods are applicable only in open areas and without characteristic corridors, and they waste resources when not required for map construction; Bluetooth-based methods have limitations in flight duration and test area; Based on convex optimization methods are computationally expensive and require centralized implementation; Some methods rely on static sources or specific movement patterns, lack practicality, and do not consider measurement noise impact. These issues prompt researchers to develop an integrated system that combines real-time relative positioning with flocking control.
IV. Method Overview The core methodological framework of this paper mainly includes three parts: collaborative relative position estimation, consistency-based fusion strategy, and distributed flocking control algorithm, detailed as follows:
Coordinated relative position estimation: Based on the UWB RCM network, we obtain information about the distance between drones, the rate of change in distance, and the relative velocity. A estimator is designed by combining continuous excitation conditions. Under bounded measurement noise, we derive the relative position estimation equation through discrete processing and analyze the boundedness of the estimation error. This strategy relies solely on the distance measurement between onboard sensors and drones, without the need for external infrastructure or pre-calibration.

Consistency-based relative position fusion: For scenarios where some drones cannot directly measure the distance to their neighbors, we utilize a relay drone to obtain an indirect relative position estimate. By designing direct and indirect estimation weights, we construct a fusion estimation equation. Under the condition of global reachability of drones, we prove the boundedness of the fusion estimation error, enhancing the positioning robustness in scenarios with packet loss.
3  
Distributed formation control algorithm: We combine the results of relative position estimation with drone-to-drone distance measurements and relative velocity estimates. Through discrete time algorithms that incorporate measurement noise, we design a discrete time formation control law for distributed formation control based on distance, adapting it to real-world drone cluster applications.
Furthermore, to ensure the practicality of our methods, we have designed a TDMA-based UWB RCM network protocol to achieve non-conflicting distance and communication interactions; we employ nonlinear regression (NLR) methodologies to initialize relative positioning, thereby improving initial estimation accuracy.
